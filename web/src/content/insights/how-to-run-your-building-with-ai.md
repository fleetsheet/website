## From Suggestion to Action

There's a distinction working its way through every real estate tech conversation in 2026: the difference between AI that tells you something and AI that does something. The first three shifts were mostly about the former — sharper sensors, better predictions, a dashboard that surfaces an anomaly and waits for someone to act on it.

Agentic AI closes that loop. Instead of flagging a problem and stopping there, it can route the work, trigger the next step, and update the record — narrowing the gap between a signal and an actual task.**Gartner predicts 33% of enterprise software applications will include agentic AI by 2028**. Gartner also predicts that at least 15% of day-to-day work decisions will be made autonomously through agentic AI by that same year.

Vendors are also shipping named agentic products: Facilio announced its "Atom" agent suite in February 2026, and Fexa announced "FexaAI" in April 2026.

## The Gap Between Piloting and Winning

Here's what the keynotes tend to skip: a 2025 survey of over 1,500 real estate decision-makers found 88% of firms already piloting AI. **Only 5% say they've hit their goals with it.** More than 60% describe their own organization as strategically, organizationally, or technically unprepared for what they've already rolled out.

An agent can be handed the authority to dispatch a technician. But if work orders live across three disconnected systems and asset history sits in a spreadsheet nobody's touched since spring, the agent has nothing dependable to act on. The gap here has less to do with a shortage of intelligence, and everything to do with a shortage of somewhere real for that intelligence to work.
